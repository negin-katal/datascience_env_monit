FLUXNET Site Explorer

Eddy-covariance towers measure the exchange of CO₂, water and energy between ecosystems and the atmosphere. Explore where the towers are, how many years of data each one has, and what the fluxes and weather look like at any site. Click a point on the map, a row in the availability chart, or a site in the table.

Where are the towers?

Each point is one tower, coloured by vegetation type (IGBP class, grouped). Hover for details, click to open its time series below.

How many years of data does each site have?

One row per site, one column per year. The shade shows the share of half-hours in that year whose CO₂ flux (NEE) was measured or gap-filled with good quality, which is a simple measure of data quality. Grey means no data.

Site time series

Select a site on the map, in the availability chart or in the table to see its data here.

Weekly means (precipitation: weekly totals) calculated from the daily FLUXNET files. A week is left empty if it has fewer than 4 days of data. Fluxes are the standard FLUXNET gap-filled products (NEE: variable u* threshold; GPP and RECO: night-time partitioning, or daytime partitioning where night-time is not available). Sign convention: negative NEE means the ecosystem takes up CO₂.

Data source and how to cite

FLUXNET data products (ONEFlux v1.3, 2026 release), downloaded with the FLUXNET Shuttle from AmeriFlux, the ICOS Carbon Portal and TERN (AmeriFlux: 22 May 2026; ICOS portal and TERN: 30 Sep 2026). The data are licensed under CC-BY-4.0. If you use them, cite each site's data product (shown in the site panel and in citations_per_site.txt), cite Pastorello et al. (2020) Sci. Data 7, 225, and include the FLUXNET acknowledgement text given in the dataset README.

FLUXNET data products were produced and harmonized by eddy covariance regional networks and data processing centers, including AmeriFlux, ChinaFlux, European Fluxes Database, ICOS, JapanFlux, KoFlux, OzFlux, SAEON, and TERN. These products also include a modified version of ERA5 hourly data provided by the Copernicus Climate Change Service. Funding for the AmeriFlux data service was provided by the U.S. Department of Energy Office of Science.
